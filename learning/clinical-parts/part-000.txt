sequence: stabilize → confirm acs/mi → distinguish stemi vs nstemi → give immediate anti-ischemic/antithrombotic treatment → reperfuse if stemi → invasive strategy if nstemi → prevent complications → secondary prevention → follow-up. current core reference is the 2025 acc/aha acute coronary syndrome guideline. (american college of cardiology) 1. initial assessment assess: abc consciousness pulse bp rr spo₂ temperature signs of heart failure signs of shock ongoing chest pain arrhythmia put patient on: cardiac monitor pulse oximeter iv access look immediately for: cardiogenic shock pulmonary edema malignant arrhythmia mechanical complication 2. immediate diagnostic test — ecg do a: 12-lead ecg within 10 minutes do not wait for troponin before acting on a clear stemi. repeat ecg if: initial ecg nondiagnostic pain persists symptoms recur. consider: right-sided leads in inferior mi posterior leads v7–v9 when posterior mi suspected. for stemi, the care pathway targets ecg acquisition within 10 minutes and immediate activation of reperfusion systems. (aha journals) 3. cardiac biomarkers request: high-sensitivity cardiac troponin use serial measurements according to the assay/protocol. mi requires: rise/fall in troponin with at least one value above the 99th percentile plus clinical/ecg/imaging evidence of ischemia. do not delay primary pci in an obvious stemi while waiting for troponin. 4. other investigations request: fbc look for: anemia infection platelet count before antithrombotics. urea, creatinine, egfr important before: contrast acei/arb anticoagulants. electrolytes especially: k⁺ mg²⁺ because abnormalities increase arrhythmia risk. random glucose + hba1c look for undiagnosed/poorly controlled dm. lipid profile preferably early during admission. lft baseline before high-intensity statin. pt/inr/aptt before anticoagulation and especially if fibrinolysis is considered. chest x-ray not to diagnose mi, but useful if: pulmonary edema pneumonia pneumothorax aortic dissection is in differential. echocardiography assess: lvef regional wall-motion abnormalities mechanical complications lv/rv function valve function pericardial effusion. 5. immediate drug treatment do not rely on the old “mona” mnemonic as a rigid sequence. modern treatment is better remembered as: aspirin + p2y12 + anticoagulant + reperfusion + symptom control + statin 6. aspirin unless contraindicated: aspirin 162–325 mg po, chewed immediately then: 75–100 mg od long term even a patient already using aspirin should receive the acute loading dose when appropriate. (aha journals) 7. add a p2y12 inhibitor this gives: dapt = aspirin + p2y12 inhibitor options depend on pci/fibrinolysis/bleeding risk. ticagrelor common pci/acs loading: 180 mg po loading then: 90 mg bd prasugrel if proceeding to pci: 60 mg loading then: 10 mg od use 5 mg od in selected low-weight/older patients. avoid prasugrel in: previous stroke/tia. clopidogrel without fibrinolysis: 300–600 mg loading then: 75 mg od with fibrinolysis: age ≤75: 300 mg loading → 75 mg od age >75: 75 mg without loading → 75 mg od the guideline provides these dosing strategies and uses dapt as standard acs therapy. (aha journals) 8. anticoagulation anticoagulation accompanies acute acs management. choice depends on: stemi vs nstemi pci vs fibrinolysis renal function bleeding risk. for pci, ufh is commonly used. a typical pci regimen is around: ufh 70–100 units/kg iv bolus if no gp iib/iiia inhibitor, adjusted by cath-lab protocol. with fibrinolysis, the corrected 2025 guideline gives: ufh 60 units/kg iv bolus, maximum 4000 units then: 12 units/kg/hour, maximum 1000 units/hour adjusted to therapeutic aptt. (aha journals) 9. oxygen give oxygen only if hypoxemic. if spo₂ \u003c90%: give supplemental o₂ and target ≥90%. if spo₂ ≥90%: routine oxygen is not recommended. this is a classic exam correction. (aha journals) 10. nitrates for persistent ischemic chest pain, hypertension or pulmonary congestion: nitroglycerin 0.4 mg sublingual may be repeated every 5 minutes up to 3 doses if bp permits. if ongoing severe ischemia/hypertension/pulmonary edema: → consider iv nitroglycerin. do not give nitrates in: hypotension suspected rv infarction with preload dependence recent pde-5 inhibitor use severe aortic stenosis. 11. morphine do not routinely give morphine to every mi patient. use only for: severe persistent pain despite nitrates/reperfusion measures because opioids can: cause hypotension delay absorption of oral p2y12 inhibitors. the modern guideline treats opioids as symptom relief rather than disease-modifying therapy. (aha journals) 12. high-intensity statin start early regardless of baseline ldl unless contraindicated. examples: atorvastatin 40–80 mg od or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg od high-intensity statin therapy is recommended for all acs patients. (aha journals) if ldl-c remains: ≥70 mg/dl (≥1.8 mmol/l) despite maximally tolerated statin: → add a nonstatin such as: ezetimibe pcsk9-directed therapy as appropriate. (american college of cardiology) 13. now distinguish stemi from nstemi this changes the reperfusion strategy completely. stemi management 14. primary pci is preferred if pci can be performed promptly: primary pci is preferred over fibrinolysis. targets: direct presentation/ems: first medical contact to device ≤90 min transfer from non-pci hospital: ≤120 min if primary pci cannot be achieved within 120 minutes, fibrinolysis becomes an important option in eligible patients presenting early. (aha journals) 15. if pci unavailable within 120 minutes if: stemi symptom onset generally \u003c12 hours no contraindication to thrombolysis pci delay >120 min then give: fibrinolytic therapy goal: door-to-lysis ≤30 minutes then transfer the patient to a pci-capable center. (aha journals) common fibrinolytics: tenecteplase alteplase. the exact fibrinolytic dose is drug- and weight-specific, so in an exam state the agent according to local protocol if not specifically asked for the dosing table. 16. after thrombolysis — assess whether it worked look for: relief of chest pain reperfusion arrhythmias significant st-segment resolution, classically >50% in the lead with greatest elevation within about 60–90 min. if fibrinolysis fails or patient becomes unstable: rescue pci urgently if fibrinolysis is successful: transfer for early coronary angiography/pci rather than simply leaving the patient in a non-pci facility. (aha journals) 17. contraindications to fibrinolysis always ask before giving. major examples: previous intracranial hemorrhage known intracranial neoplasm/vascular lesion recent ischemic stroke suspected aortic dissection active major bleeding significant recent head trauma severe uncontrolled hypertension. if fibrinolysis contraindicated: transfer for pci. nstemi management 18. do not thrombolyse nstemi this is a major exam point. nstemi = no fibrinolysis. treat with: aspirin p2y12 inhibitor according to strategy anticoagulation statin anti-ischemic therapy risk-stratified invasive coronary angiography. 19. risk stratify nstemi use: grace score and/or timi. very high risk includes: cardiogenic shock refractory/recurrent ischemia acute hf malignant arrhythmia hemodynamic instability. these patients need: immediate invasive strategy, generally \u003c2 hours. high risk: grace >140 dynamic st changes significant/rising troponin → coronary angiography ideally: within 24 hours. intermediate risk: → invasive evaluation during admission, commonly before discharge and often within 72 hours. (aha journals) 20. beta blocker if stable and no contraindications: start an oral beta blocker within 24 hours. example: metoprolol tartrate 25–50 mg po bd then titrate according to hr/bp/lv function. avoid early beta blockade in: acute pulmonary edema cardiogenic shock/low-output state marked bradycardia hypotension 2nd/3rd-degree av block without pacing active severe bronchospasm. early oral beta blockers reduce reinfarction and ventricular arrhythmias. (aha journals) 21. ace inhibitor / arb particularly important if: lvef ≤40% anterior mi hypertension diabetes heart failure. examples: enalapril 2.5 mg bd or ramipril 1.25–2.5 mg bd then titrate as tolerated. if acei intolerant: → arb. monitor: bp k⁺ creatinine. the 2025 guideline recommends acei/arb in these high-risk acs groups. (aha journals) 22. mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist if: lvef ≤40% and hf symptoms and/or diabetes consider: eplerenone or appropriate mra. use if: adequate renal function no significant hyperkalemia. the 2025 guideline gives this a strong recommendation in this post-mi population. (aha journals) 23. if cardiogenic shock develops look for: hypotension cold extremities oliguria altered mentation high lactate pulmonary edema. management: icu/cardiology urgent coronary angiography and revascularization cautious hemodynamic support norepinephrine if significant hypotension inotrope such as dobutamine in selected low-output patients mechanical circulatory support in selected refractory shock. the key treatment in mi-related cardiogenic shock is: prompt revascularization. (american college of cardiology) 24. treat acute pulmonary edema if mi complicated by acute lv failure: sit upright oxygen if hypoxemic nitrates if bp allows iv loop diuretic if congested niv if respiratory distress urgent reperfusion. do not give a large fluid load unless there is a specific preload problem such as rv infarction. 25. right ventricular infarction think about it in inferior stemi. clues: hypotension raised jvp clear lungs inferior stemi. confirm with right-sided ecg leads. management differs: avoid nitrates if preload dependent/hypotensive avoid aggressive diuresis cautious iv fluid may be needed urgent reperfusion. 26. arrhythmias vf/pulseless vt immediate defibrillation + als. symptomatic bradycardia atropine ± pacing according to severity. sustained vt treat according to stability and acls principles. af control rate/rhythm and assess anticoagulation as appropriate. 27. mechanical complications very important after mi. think: papillary muscle rupture → acute severe mr → pulmonary edema + new systolic murmur. ventricular septal rupture → new harsh pansystolic murmur + shock. free-wall rupture → tamponade/pea/sudden death. lv aneurysm → hf, arrhythmia, thrombus. these require: urgent echocardiography + cardiothoracic/cardiology intervention. 28. complications of mi for exams, divide them: electrical vt vf af av block sudden cardiac death. pump failure acute lv failure pulmonary edema cardiogenic shock. mechanical papillary muscle rupture vsd free-wall rupture. inflammatory early pericarditis dressler syndrome. thromboembolic lv mural thrombus systemic embolism/stroke. structural lv aneurysm chronic ischemic cardiomyopathy chronic hf. recurrent ischemia reinfarction recurrent angina. 29. dapt duration for acs patients without high bleeding risk: aspirin + p2y12 inhibitor for at least 12 months is the default strategy. then usually: continue aspirin long term. shorter/modified strategies may be used when bleeding risk is high. (american college of cardiology) 30. prevention after mi primary prevention before mi occurs: control hypertension control diabetes treat dyslipidemia smoking cessation maintain healthy weight regular exercise healthy diet manage ckd avoid cocaine/stimulant use treat established stable cad. 31. secondary prevention patient already had mi: aspirin appropriate dapt duration high-intensity statin beta blocker when indicated acei/arb when indicated mra when indicated bp control dm control smoking cessation weight control cardiac rehabilitation exercise after stabilization diet modification medication adherence. 32. exercise / cardiac rehabilitation before discharge: refer to cardiac rehabilitation. this reduces: mortality recurrent mi hospital readmission and improves functional capacity. (american college of cardiology) exercise prescription should be individualized after stabilization, commonly progressing toward: ~150 minutes/week moderate aerobic activity rather than telling the patient merely “walk.” 33. follow-up after discharge, review early—often within: 1–2 weeks especially after a complicated mi or new medication initiation. assess: recurrent chest pain dyspnea orthopnea edema palpitations/syncope bp hr medication adherence bleeding on dapt statin adverse effects acei/mra effects. 34. laboratory follow-up check as appropriate: creatinine k⁺ after acei/arb/mra hb if bleeding suspected glucose/hba1c lipid profile. the 2025 guideline recommends repeating a fasting lipid panel: 4–8 weeks after starting or adjusting lipid-lowering therapy. (american college of cardiology) 35. repeat echocardiography if lvef is reduced after mi: reassess after adequate recovery and guideline-directed therapy when the result will influence management. if persistent: lvef ≤35% despite appropriate therapy consider icd eligibility for primary prevention after the appropriate waiting period. do not implant a primary-prevention icd immediately after an
