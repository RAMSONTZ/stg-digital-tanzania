previous year, rather than requiring the older higher exacerbation threshold. 1. initial assessment first determine whether the patient is stable or presenting with an acute exacerbation of copd — aecopd. assess: abc consciousness respiratory rate spo₂ pulse bp temperature ability to speak accessory muscle use cyanosis chest expansion/air entry wheeze signs of exhaustion peripheral edema/jvp mental state look specifically for acute respiratory failure: severe dyspnea at rest rr markedly elevated accessory muscle use cyanosis reduced air entry/silent chest confusion/drowsiness hypoxemia hypercapnia/acidosis. also look for diseases that can mimic or precipitate aecopd: pneumonia pulmonary embolism acute heart failure pneumothorax acs/arrhythmia. 2. confirm the diagnosis of copd diagnosis requires compatible clinical history plus spirometry. ask about: chronic dyspnea chronic cough sputum production recurrent lower respiratory infections cigarette smoking biomass exposure occupational dust/fumes previous tb childhood respiratory disease family history. spirometry diagnosis is confirmed when: post-bronchodilator fev₁/fvc \u003c0.70 then grade airflow obstruction using occasional. 5. group b 0 moderate/severe exacerbations mmrc ≥2 or caat ≥10 treatment: laba + lama this is preferred over starting a single long-acting agent in most group b patients. 6. group e ≥1 patient has concomitant asthma, an ics-containing regimen is required. 7. drug classes to know saba — short-acting beta-2 agonist salbutamol rescue medication. practical acute nebulized dose: 2.5–5 mg adverse effects: tremor tachycardia palpitations hypokalemia lactic acidosis with excessive dosing. 8. sama ipratropium bromide practical acute nebulized dose: 500 micrograms nebulized commonly every laba examples: formoterol salmeterol indacaterol olodaterol. long-term bronchodilation. do not use laba alone as a rescue treatment. 10. lama examples: tiotropium common maintenance regimen: 18
